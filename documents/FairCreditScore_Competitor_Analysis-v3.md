## 1. Executive Summary

India's credit assessment landscape is structured into four distinct layers, each with different incumbents, strengths, and gaps. Understanding this structure is critical to positioning FairCreditScore correctly:

- **Layer 1 — Traditional Credit Bureaus:** TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, CRIF Highmark, and Equifax. They own the legacy credit graph but cannot score the credit-invisible.
- **Layer 2 — Bank Statement / AA Infrastructure:** Perfios, FinBox, Setu, Finvu, OneMoney, Decentro, Precisa. They move data; they don't produce a portable score.
- **Layer 3 — Alternative-Data Lenders:** KreditBee, Slice, Lendingkart, Indifi, CredAble. They use alternative data internally to underwrite their own loan books — they do not sell scores.
- **Layer 4 — Standalone Alternative Scoring Platforms:** a thin and fragmented field. This is where FairCreditScore plays — and wins.

Our wedge is unambiguous: a portable, lender-agnostic, explainable alternative credit score, designed for the borrower as much as for the lender, anchored on UPI-first behavioural data, and engineered for DPDP Act and RBI 2026 compliance from day one.

| Layer | What They Do | Examples | Gap We Fill |

## 4. Layer-by-Layer Competitor Profiles

### 4.1 Layer 1 — Traditional Credit Bureaus

#### TransUnion CIBIL

The dominant credit bureau in India. Preferred by major banks (HDFC, SBI, ICICI, Axis). Has launched CIBIL Score for the Unbanked and alternative scoring add-ons that incorporate utility, rent, and telco data. However, the core product remains anchored to traditional credit-history reporting.

- **Strengths:** Brand trust, regulatory licence, decades of repayment data, deep bank relationships.
- **Weaknesses:** Slow innovation cycle, score remains opaque to borrowers, alternative-data products bolted on rather than designed-in, limited UPI-native modelling.
- **Threat to FairCreditScore:** Could launch a serious alternative-data product. *Mitigation:* speed of execution, borrower-side brand, explainability moat.

#### Experian India

Preferred by digital lenders and fintech NBFCs because of dynamic scoring updates and global analytics. Already experiments with rent, utility, and mobile recharge data for thin-file borrowers.

- **Strengths:** Most innovation-friendly of the four bureaus, fintech-friendly APIs, recent-behaviour weighting.
- **Weaknesses:** Still ultimately a credit bureau — the model is calibrated on borrowers who already have a credit footprint. Limited UPI-native data ingestion.

#### CRIF Highmark

Strongest in microfinance, MSME, and rural lending. Trusted by NBFCs, MFIs, and small finance banks. Closer to our target population than CIBIL or Equifax.

- **Strengths:** Microfinance depth, rural reach, MSME-grade reporting, MFI relationships.
- **Weaknesses:** Still credit-history dependent; limited consumer-facing tooling; no UPI-first scoring product.

#### Equifax India

Smallest of the four by domestic share. Mostly serves enterprise lenders. Limited innovation in alternative scoring.

> **Aggregate Layer 1 view:** All four bureaus understand the alternative-data opportunity, but their core scoring product is a 60-year-old paradigm with new data sources duct-taped on. None of them is built UPI-first, none gives the borrower a transparent explanation, and none is structurally designed for the credit-invisible.

### 4.3 Layer 3 — Vertically Integrated Alternative-Data Lenders

These are not bureaus or scoring platforms — they are lenders that built proprietary alternative-data underwriting to feed their own loan books. They demonstrate market validation but do not compete with us directly. They are also future customers.

| Lender | Focus | How They Validate Our Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| KreditBee | Personal loans for young, thin-file borrowers | Uses mobile data, UPI behaviour, and transaction patterns. Proves the segment is bankable. |
| Slice | Credit-card alternative for young Indians | Showed alt-data underwriting can achieve credit-card-grade NPAs. |
| Lendingkart | MSME working capital | Uses GST and digital payment trails — exactly the data we ingest. |
| Indifi | MSME secured + unsecured | Cash-flow lending model; complementary borrower base. |
| CredAble | MSME working capital, supply-chain finance | Data-backed underwriting and cash-flow assessment. |
| Falcon (CreditFX) | Credit-line-on-UPI infrastructure for SFBs and mid-banks | Validates the credit-on-UPI thesis at scale. |

> **Strategic note:** Each of these lenders runs alt-data scoring as an internal cost centre. As FairCreditScore matures, several will be candidates to either license our score (cheaper than maintaining their own model) or to use us as a second-opinion challenger model.

### 4.4 Layer 4 — Standalone Alternative Scoring Platforms

This is the category in which FairCreditScore directly competes. The category is genuinely fragmented and immature in India. Most players today are either (a) credit-bureau add-ons, (b) lender-internal models repackaged as APIs, or (c) global vendors not deeply localised to India Stack. There is no clear, branded, borrower-trusted alternative score.

Globally, the closest analogue is FICO XD or Experian Boost — but neither has a meaningful India-localised, UPI-native equivalent. Domestically, RBL, Airtel Finance, and a handful of fintechs publish whitepapers about alternative scoring without offering it as a portable, lender-agnostic product.

#### Our Direct Competitive Reality

- There is no dominant standalone alternative-credit-score brand in India today.
- Lenders are buying point solutions (AA pipes, bank statement analysers, internal scorecards) and stitching them together — at significant cost and time.
- Borrowers cannot see, understand, or carry an alternative score across institutions.

**This is the white-space that FairCreditScore is built to occupy.**

## 6. SWOT Analysis

### ✅ Strengths

- UPI-first architecture aligned with India's dominant payment rail
- Explainable AI (SHAP) by default — meets emerging regulatory expectations
- Borrower-facing brand and dashboard — no incumbent has this
- DPDP Act 2023 compliant from day one (right to erasure, right to explanation)
- Multilingual, mobile-first design for Tier 2/3 India
- Lender-agnostic, portable score — true two-sided value
- Designed for the credit-invisible — addressable market of 400M+

### ⚠️ Weaknesses

- No prior brand recognition vs CIBIL or Experian
- No existing lender distribution at launch — partnerships need to be built
- Score has no track record yet — needs proven backtests with lender partners
- Initial dependency on NBFC anchor partner for FIU registration
- Capital-intensive scaling to reach the 5M-borrower threshold
- AA coverage gap (~38% of borrowers) requires hybrid PDF fallback at extra cost

### 🚀 Opportunities

- ₹30 lakh crore MSME credit gap — multi-decade addressable market
- Government of India actively evaluating AI-based credit scoring (Feb 2026)
- Credit Line on UPI (CLOU) creating new real-time scoring use cases
- AA framework maturing — coverage expanding rapidly
- RBI Regulatory Sandbox provides safe path to test innovative scoring models
- Cross-sell into insurance and wealth scoring later
- Indian DPI exportable — UAE, SE Asia, Africa diaspora corridors
- Embedded scoring SDK for e-commerce, gig platforms, EdTech, healthcare loans

### 🔴 Threats

- Credit bureaus (CIBIL, Experian) launch their own native UPI-data products
- Large fintechs (PhonePe, Paytm, Google Pay) launch in-app scoring as a feature
- RBI tightens norms on non-CIC alternative scoring
- DPDP Act compliance failure → ₹250 crore penalty exposure
- Macroeconomic stress reduces lender appetite for new scoring partnerships
- Synthetic transaction fraud (gaming UPI flows) erodes model accuracy
- AA framework changes (data retention windows, fee structure) disrupt economics

- We are **not a lender**. We will never hold a loan book.

- We are **not an AA** — we use AAs as a pipe, not as a competitor.
- We do **not collect contacts, SMS, location, or social-media data** — explicitly avoided per RBI 2026 norms.

## 9. Conclusion

India's credit market is undergoing the most significant structural shift in a generation. UPI has digitised payments; the Account Aggregator framework has digitised consent; the DPDP Act has formalised data rights; and CLOU is moving credit itself onto the UPI rail. Yet the credit assessment layer — the connective tissue between a borrower and a lender — remains anchored to a 1960s American paradigm of *"have you borrowed before?"*

This is the seam in the market. The traditional bureaus are large, profitable, and slow. The infrastructure layer is rich but fragmented. The vertically integrated lenders are scaling but cannot license their internal scores. The standalone alternative-scoring category is wide open.

FairCreditScore is purpose-built for this seam. We are UPI-native, borrower-trusted, lender-agnostic, regulator-aligned, and explainability-first. Our wedge is defensible, our market is vast, and our timing — with regulatory tailwinds, infrastructure maturity, and proven CLOU-era unit economics — is rare.

The credit-invisible 400 million Indians do not need pity or paperwork. They need a fair score that reflects how they actually live and earn. **That is the product we are building.**
